We've been featured in WTiN's latest Innovation Outlook

We're delighted to share that Brilliant Dyes has been profiled in the newest edition of WTiN's Innovation Outlook — the H2 2026 special report on Dyes, Inks & Finishes. Our algae-derived colour platform is featured alongside a selection of the innovators reshaping how the textile industry approaches colour and finishing.

Find out more about WTiN

WTiN (the World Textile Information Network) is a leading global source of intelligence, analysis and insight for the textile and apparel industry. Its quarterly Innovation Outlook series tracks the new and emerging technologies set to shape the value chain, profiling both established suppliers and early-stage disruptors.

This edition turns its attention to one of manufacturing's most resource-intensive stages: dyeing and finishing. It explores how cleaner, smarter approaches — from waterless and low-liquor dyeing to biobased chemistries, precision digital colouration and intelligent process control — are lowering the environmental footprint of textile colour while improving performance.

You can learn more about WTiN and request access to the full report at wtin.com.

What's included in our feature

The report introduces us as an Imperial College London spin-out producing natural colour from microalgae. A few of the things it covers:

  • —Our approach to colour. Rather than relying on genetically modified organisms, we cultivate naturally abundant, colour-rich algae in bioreactors — including cyanobacteria such as spirulina — to derive our range of shades.
  • —Our four base colours. From Brilliant Blue, Brilliant Green, Brilliant Red and Brilliant Yellow, we use AI-based modelling to blend a wide spectrum of vibrant, tunable colours with no synthetic ingredients.
  • —Beyond textiles. Because the algae we use already sit on existing safe-materials lists, our dyes are suitable for cosmetics and food applications as well as textiles.
  • —Our sustainability case. The feature highlights the reduced water, energy and carbon footprint of our process, the carbon-fixing nature of algae cultivation, and the biodegradability of our finished colourants. It also picks up on how we valorise our leftover biomass into secondary markets such as animal feed and biostimulants.
  • —Where we are today. It covers our current bioreactor and extraction setup, the digital twin we use to model and validate the process, our partnership with Heriot-Watt University on dye fixation, and our plans to scale toward pilot capacity.

WTiN placed our platform at the "Emerging" stage on its Commercial Readiness Scale — reflecting a technology moving from research toward pilot scale.
